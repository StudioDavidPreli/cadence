# V01 Overview Video: Scripts

**Working doc, 2026-08-14.** Two treatments of the V01 overview video's words (see `visual-aid-checklist.md`, Priority 1): a card script (on-screen text only) and a voiceover take. Both run product voice with one first-person beat at the close; the two takes use different closing lines on purpose, so a mixed edit never doubles a line. A third take, product voice throughout with no first person, is in `v01-script-product.md`.

## Take one: cards

Timecodes follow David's cut (0:00 to 1:14). Card-in times are suggestions; slide them to the edit. Reading pace assumed ~12 characters per second; every card fits its scene with room.

Rule carried over from the checklist: in-video type (the title, the lane labels, the "export" sign, the format labels) does the naming. No card repeats a word already on screen in its scene.

## Why the cards sit where they do

- **C01 to C03 are one argument in three beats.** Someone decides; today they decide by hand, slowly; a token gives the decision a name. The snap to 100 is the payoff and needs no caption.
- **C05 echoes the landing hero** ("the interface answers in real time"). The video and the site should speak identically where they overlap.
- **C06 borrows the capture note's own phrasing** (the presets-explore rig scene "ending on an Explore state no preset could hold").
- **C08 and C09 split the fidelity claim** so neither card runs long: first the claim, then the mechanism.
- **C11 is the runtime line** for the run-button scene: the runner pun, and the fact underneath it (the app ships one Rive runtime).
- **C13 drops "like a wave."** The diagonal stagger is the wave; the card trusts the shot.
- **C16 is the export system's actual property**: all four formats derive from one function, so they cannot disagree.
- **C17 is the single first-person card.** It recontextualizes the boulder on the match cut: the wall was the first job, this tool is the second. The URL, not a sentence, gets the last word.

## Alternates, if a card fights the edit

- C02: "Nudged by hand, value by value."
- C11: "Fifty runners, one runtime." (if the tile count reads better here than at C12)
- C17: "I built Cadence to stop explaining this intersection and start showing it." (longer; needs the full four seconds)

### Read notes

- The opening block ends on "two milliseconds at a time" while the slider is still crawling; let the pause sit there, then hit "A token gives that decision a name" as the counter snaps to 100.
- The 0:00 sentence pair quotes the Plain Overview's opening ("how much it gives and how fast it returns") so the film and the page speak identically.
- 0:22 and 0:25 are quick back-to-back lines over a busy scene; if the read feels crowded, drop 0:25 and let the tool bar carry Explore alone.
- 0:45 is the runtime line. Alternate if the pun reads too cute aloud: "The whole field runs on one runtime."
- The last line clears by about 1:12; the mantis, the boulder, and the end title play out unnarrated.
- Alternate close, if the intersection line feels distant from the footage: "I spent eight years handing specs across the wall. This is the other side." (The first sentence is C17's; don't run both takes' closers in one edit.)
- If cards and VO mix, the rule is one channel per scene: a card and a spoken line saying different things in the same seconds is two captions fighting.
